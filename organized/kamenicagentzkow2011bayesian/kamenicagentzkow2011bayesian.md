# Bayesian Persuasion

## Key Takeaways for My Research

**Main Contribution:**
This paper introduces a foundational model of strategic information provision under commitment, showing how a sender can persuade a rational Bayesian receiver by designing the receiver's informational environment. The key insight is that persuasion is possible even when the receiver is fully rational and understands the sender's incentive to manipulate, because Bayesian rationality only constrains the *expectation* of posterior beliefs, not their distribution.

**Methodology/Technique:**
The paper uses a convex analysis approach, reformulating the sender's problem of choosing an optimal signal as a search over Bayes-plausible distributions of posterior beliefs. The key mathematical tool is the concave closure of the sender's value function, which characterizes the maximum attainable payoff.

**Relevance to My Work:**
This connects directly to my interest in information economics and belief formation. The model provides a clean theoretical framework for understanding how strategic agents design information structures in settings with information asymmetry. The commitment assumption parallels scenarios in market design (like school ratings) or institutional settings (like clinical trial registration) where information-gathering procedures are observable. The comparative statics on preference alignment relate to questions about how cooperation norms affect information transmission.

## Quick Reference
Models how a sender can design a signal (information structure) to persuade a rational Bayesian receiver, showing that persuasion is generically possible when the receiver's action space is finite and the default action is not sender-optimal.
